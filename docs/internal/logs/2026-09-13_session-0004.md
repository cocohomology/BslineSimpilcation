## Main result

Yes. On the union partition of breakpoints, write
\[
e(t)=\alpha_i+\beta_i(t-x_i).
\]
The endpoint-preserving error is piecewise cubic:
\[
E(t)=P_i+V_i h+\frac12\alpha_i h^2+\frac16\beta_i h^3,
\qquad h=t-x_i.
\]
The only global coupling is the initial slope
\[
E'(a)=-\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b(b-s)e(s)\,ds,
\]
a single vector moment. After that, \(E,E'\) propagate in one forward pass.

For vector-valued curves, on each span
\[
F(t)=\|E(t)\|^2
\]
has degree at most six and
\[
F'(t)=2E(t)\cdot E'(t)
\]
has degree at most five. The quintic degree is generic.

Therefore the exact global maximum is obtained from:
- all span boundaries;
- all real roots inside each span of \(E\cdot E'=0\).

This produces independent fixed-degree univariate root problems. Structurally, with \(n\) union spans, the evaluation is \(O(n)\) fixed-degree subproblems, though precision/bit cost can grow near multiple roots.

Detailed derivation: `docs/internal/derivations/pl_green_evaluation.md`.

## Second certification route

A cubic-error span can be written in Bernstein/Bézier form. By the convex-hull property and convexity of Euclidean norm,
\[
\|E(u)\|\le \max_j\|Q_j\|
\]
for its four Bézier control vectors.

This gives a certified upper bound. De Casteljau subdivision tightens it, while evaluated curve points give lower bounds. Thus a branch-and-bound tolerance test can certify \(N_G\le\varepsilon\) or \(N_G>\varepsilon\) to arbitrary precision without dense uniform sampling.

An exact equality case may require a root-isolation fallback.

## Comparison with Hausdorff

The synchronized Green calculation is equivalent to finding the farthest point of the piecewise-cubic error curve \(E(t)\) from the fixed origin: a one-parameter problem.

A directed Hausdorff distance has the nested structure
\[
\max_t\min_u\|C(t)-\widetilde C(u)\|,
\]
with nearest-point branch changes and segment-pair coupling.

This is not a formal runtime theorem against all possible Hausdorff algorithms, but the structural reduction is genuine: \(N_G\) requires only local univariate fixed-degree extrema after one global moment.

## Research consequence

The concern that the Green metric might be too difficult to evaluate has been substantially removed.

At the synchronized level, both major ingredients are now exact in the \(C''\) domain:
- representation complexity;
- positional error.

Moreover the positional error is directly certifiable at low algebraic degree. Therefore global \(L^p\) norms are no longer needed as the primary synchronized metric merely for computational convenience. They may remain useful later for pruning or comparison with Lyche-style methods.
