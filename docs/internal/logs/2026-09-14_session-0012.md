## Goal

Return from verification theory to the original bottleneck:
constructing a low-complexity approximation of q=C''.

## Main observation

The simplification problem should not be posed as ordinary PL approximation:

  ||q-q_tilde||

but through the induced displacement metric:

  ||G_D(q-q_tilde)||_infinity.

The reason is that the final geometric error depends on the double integration operator. High-frequency local oscillations in q may have little effect after integration, while low-frequency bias may dominate.

## Current hypothesis

A promising direction is to investigate whether q-space simplification under the Green-induced metric has a fundamentally different structure from classical knot removal or Lp fitting.

Potential consequences:

- local kinks in q may be removable if their integrated effect is small;
- complexity should be measured by modes important after integration rather than raw PL complexity;
- fixed partition experiments may reveal whether the metric itself gives an advantage before introducing free knots.

## Review / caution

This is a research hypothesis, not a result.

Open questions:

1. Does Green metric actually lead to better simplification than existing methods?
2. Can the induced optimization problem be solved efficiently?
3. Is this merely a rephrasing of existing spline approximation theory?
