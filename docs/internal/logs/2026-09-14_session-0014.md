## Correction of Sessions 0012–0013

Those sessions promoted the next question to q-space optimization under
\[
\|q-\widetilde q\|_G=\|G_D(q-\widetilde q)\|_\infty.
\]
The observation is valid, but the route was premature.

For the fixed endpoint reconstruction map \(R\),
\[
R(q)-R(\widetilde q)=G_D(q-\widetilde q),
\]
so
\[
\|q-\widetilde q\|_G
=\|R(q)-R(\widetilde q)\|_\infty.
\]
Thus the Green norm is exactly the synchronized positional uniform norm pulled back to the second-derivative coordinate. Its high-frequency damping interpretation is useful intuition, but it does not by itself prove a new approximation structure or justify skipping the unfinished verifier.

Decision: mark Sessions 0012–0013 as exploratory/superseded as the active main line. Preserve their ideas under the theory-seed/candidate-generation branch for later.

### Literature sanity check

This correction is also consistent with the Lyche–Mørken data-reduction paper. Their Section 2 explicitly formulates the reduction problem after **choosing a norm**, notes that errors can in principle be measured in any norm, and seeks a low-dimensional spline subspace without claiming that their algorithm always finds the true lowest-dimensional one. Therefore merely replacing an \(L^p\)-type error by a Green-pulled norm is not, by itself, a new data-reduction architecture. Any later q-space breakthrough must come from additional structure—locality, moments, combinatorics, or a better candidate family—not from renaming the positional norm.

The same literature review also reinforces the parameterization caveat of the parked Hermite idea: de Boor–Höllig–Sabin explicitly describe standard cubic Hermite interpolation as simple and local but parameterization-dependent and generally only \(C^1\). That is exactly the tradeoff our later candidate-generation work would need to evaluate rather than hide.

## Main theoretical advance of this session

Let
\[
P(t)=\widetilde C(t),\qquad Q(u)=C(u),
\]
inside one already-certified cubic-cubic box, and let \(u=\sigma(t)\) be the unique local normal branch satisfying
\[
F(t,u):=(P(t)-Q(u))\cdot Q'(u)=0.
\]

Define
\[
D(t,u)=\|P(t)-Q(u)\|^2,
\qquad
\psi(t)=D(t,\sigma(t)).
\]
Since
\[
D_u=-2F,
\]
we have \(D_u=0\) on the branch, so the chain rule collapses to
\[
\boxed{
\psi'(t)=D_t(t,\sigma(t))
=2(P(t)-Q(\sigma(t)))\cdot P'(t).
}
\]

Therefore every interior extremum of the deviation along the branch satisfies
\[
\boxed{
(P(t)-Q(u))\cdot Q'(u)=0,
\qquad
(P(t)-Q(u))\cdot P'(t)=0.
}

The connecting vector is a common normal of the two curve spans.

For cubics, the two equations have fixed bidegrees at most
\[
(3,5)\quad\text{and}\quad(5,3).
\]
The squared distance has bidegree at most \((6,6)\).

Hence, generically, exact branch-max certification reduces to finitely many fixed-degree bivariate polynomial intersections plus branch/box boundaries. A cheap preliminary certificate is simply a Bernstein upper bound of \(D\) over the whole certified box.

## Important geometric correction prompted by the feedback

The experiment's observation that a certified local normal foot need not be the global nearest point is **not** a defect for one-sided upper-bound certification.

For any certified correspondence point,
\[
\operatorname{dist}(P(t),\operatorname{Im}Q)
\le
\|P(t)-Q(\sigma(t))\|.
\]
So global-nearest uniqueness is stronger than needed for the candidate-to-reference directed distance.

However, one local branch does not automatically provide a symmetric Hausdorff upper bound unless its image covers the intended reference interval. The local box certificate alone does not prove whole-curve coverage or stitching.

This makes the next theoretical gap sharper:

> not global nearest-point uniqueness, but global assembly / coverage of the local correspondences.

## Parked insight for later candidate generation

During the re-analysis, one potentially useful q-space structure emerged and was deliberately **not** promoted to the current main line.

On an interval \([\alpha,\beta]\), replacing \(q=C''\) by the unique affine function \(\ell\) satisfying
\[
\int_\alpha^\beta(q-\ell)=0,
\qquad
\int_\alpha^\beta(\beta-s)(q-\ell)\,ds=0
\]
produces, after integration from the left endpoint, the standard cubic Hermite interpolant matching \(C,C'\) at both interval endpoints. The resulting position/tangent error vanishes at both ends and does not propagate outside the block.

This could later support block deletion / shortest-path coarsening and naturally handles simultaneous deletion of several knots. It is recorded as a theory seed only; its derivative interpolation and generic C1/double-knot restriction may be too conservative.
